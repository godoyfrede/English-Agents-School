# CEFR Level Descriptions — Reference Guide

## A1 — Beginner

**Can do:**
- Understand and use familiar everyday expressions
- Introduce themselves and answer basic personal questions
- Interact in a simple way if the other person speaks slowly

**Typical vocabulary:** Numbers, colors, family, days of the week, basic objects
**Grammar:** "to be", "to have", simple present, basic questions
**Example sentences:** "My name is Fred." / "I am from Brazil." / "I like pizza."

---

## A2 — Elementary

**Can do:**
- Understand sentences and frequently used expressions in familiar areas
- Communicate in simple, routine tasks
- Describe their background and immediate environment

**Typical vocabulary:** Food, shopping, transport, work basics, feelings
**Grammar:** Simple past, simple future (will/going to), modal verbs (can, must), articles
**Example sentences:** "Yesterday I went to the store." / "I can speak a little English."

---

## B1 — Intermediate

**Can do:**
- Understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters
- Deal with most travel situations
- Produce simple connected text on topics of personal interest

**Typical vocabulary:** Travel, current events, opinions, abstract concepts (basic)
**Grammar:** Present perfect, past continuous, conditionals (type 1 & 2), phrasal verbs (common)
**Example sentences:** "I have lived here for five years." / "If I had more time, I'd travel more."

---

## B2 — Upper-Intermediate

**Can do:**
- Understand the main ideas of complex texts on concrete and abstract topics
- Interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity
- Produce clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects

**Typical vocabulary:** Professional language, nuanced opinions, news media, academic topics
**Grammar:** Past perfect, passive voice, relative clauses, discourse markers, conditionals (type 3)
**Example sentences:** "The project, which had been delayed, was finally completed." / "She would have succeeded had she tried harder."

---

## C1 — Advanced

**Can do:**
- Understand a wide range of demanding, longer texts
- Express ideas fluently and spontaneously without much searching for expressions
- Use language flexibly and effectively for social, academic and professional purposes

**Typical vocabulary:** Idioms, collocations, academic vocabulary, nuanced synonyms
**Grammar:** Complex structures, inversion, cleft sentences, advanced modals
**Example sentences:** "Not only did he finish first, but he also broke the record." / "Had I known, I would have acted differently."

---

## C2 — Proficient

**Can do:**
- Understand virtually everything heard or read
- Summarize information from different spoken and written sources
- Express themselves spontaneously, very fluently and precisely

**Note:** This level is equivalent to an educated native speaker. Very rare for non-native learners.

---

## Writing Evaluation Rubric (0–20 per exercise)

| Criterion | 5 pts | 3–4 pts | 1–2 pts | 0 pts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | Varied, precise, level-appropriate | Adequate, some repetition | Limited, frequent errors | Very poor |
| **Grammar** | Correct structures, minor slips | Some errors, mostly clear | Frequent errors, meaning affected | Incomprehensible |
| **Coherence** | Logical flow, well-connected | Mostly logical, some gaps | Disjointed, hard to follow | No logical structure |
| **Complexity** | Rich structures, sophisticated | Some complex structures | Mostly simple | Only fragments |

## Conversation Evaluation Rubric (0–30 total)

| Criterion | Excellent (9–10) | Good (6–8) | Developing (3–5) | Beginning (0–2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fluency** | Natural rhythm, minimal hesitation | Some hesitation but clear | Frequent pauses, choppy | Very halting |
| **Vocabulary** | Rich, varied, idiomatic | Adequate, some searching | Limited, repetitive | Very restricted |
| **Grammar** | Accurate, wide range | Some errors, not impeding | Errors sometimes impede | Frequently unclear |
